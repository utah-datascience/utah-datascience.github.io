Time series are ubiquitous, underpinning critical systems across domains such as energy, healthcare, manufacturing, and transportation. Detecting anomalous patterns in these data is essential for identifying failures and ensuring system reliability. Despite decades of research, however, time-series anomaly detection remains challenging due to the diverse and complex nature of anomalies, the variability of real-world data, and the difficulty of developing solutions that generalize across applications without extensive manual configuration.

In this talk, I will discuss these challenges and present our vision towards automated and reliable anomaly detection through Chameleon, an ongoing research project supported by an NSF CAREER Award. I will first introduce HYDRA (SIGMOD 2026), a standalone detector that recognizes diverse and complex anomaly patterns while substantially improving detection accuracy and maintaining fast inference. I will then discuss our efforts towards automated solutions, highlighting SENSE (VLDB 2025), MLLM4TS (TMLR 2026), and ongoing work in this direction. Finally, I will highlight our contributions to more reliable evaluation through TSB-AD (NeurIPS 2024), a comprehensive anomaly detection benchmark, and VUS (VLDB 2022), an evaluation measure widely adopted by the community. Together, these efforts aim to advance anomaly detection towards solutions that are accurate, efficient, automated, and reliable across diverse real-world applications.
